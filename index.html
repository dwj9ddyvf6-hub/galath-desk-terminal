Architecture · in plain English

How the desk works

Everything behind this page: where the data comes from, how a buy signal becomes a paper trade, how the desk keeps itself running, and how this site is updated.

The desk is a set of rules-based trading strategies (we call each one a sleeve) running on two Alpaca paper-trading accounts with simulated money. A single program, Lazarus, reads fresh buy signals, checks each sleeve's rules, sends limit orders, records every step in a tamper-evident ledger, and publishes the results here.

The whole system

From data to trade to this page

Read top to bottom. Each box is one part of the system; the arrows show which way information flows.

Step 1

Where the data comes from

Yahoo Finance price barsDaily, weekly and hourly price history for thousands of US stocks. This is what the signal engine reads.
AlpacaThe broker. It supplies live quotes (used to price each order) and holds the two paper accounts where orders are filled.
Alt-dataCompany insiders buying their own stock (SEC filings), dark-pool trading volume (FINRA), and Wikipedia page views. Collected every night.
Options snapshotsA nightly snapshot of the options market, used to estimate dealer gamma (how options dealers' hedging may push a stock around).
ChartsLectorThe public screens and market gauges of the trader @ChartsLector, rebuilt from his open-source scripts. Two Test-desk sleeves trade his screens; the ChartsLector tab shows the gauges.
Tensor and MidnaTensor is the desk's signal engine: it scores every stock with 7 trend and momentum votes and produces the Cyan, Green and Swing buy signals. Midna is a separate strategy library whose books run as Test-desk sleeves.

Step 2 and 3

One loop runs everything

Lazarus is one always-on program that wakes every 20 seconds and asks "is anything due?". On a trading day it runs the research jobs that turn data into buy signals, then a desk check at 9:35, 10:31, 11:31, 12:31, 1:31, 2:31, 3:31, 3:42 and 4:01, 4:50 ET. Each check handles both accounts and every sleeve.

Every dot is a scheduled event. If the computer was asleep, Lazarus runs a single catch-up check when it wakes, never a burst of missed ones.

Inside a desk check

How a signal becomes a trade

  1. Signal. A research job reports a buy signal for a stock (for example a weekly Cyan signal). Each sleeve only listens for its own kind of signal.
  2. Entry rule. Most sleeves wait for a fear day: in the last 40 to 10 minutes before the close, QQQ (the Nasdaq-100 fund) and the stock must both be below yesterday's close, and the price no more than 2% above the signal-day close. They get 5 trading days to see one; otherwise the signal is dropped. Hourly sleeves use the same idea within 30 minutes of the signal. Midna's sleeves skip a buy when QQQ is down, VIX is up and the stock is down at the same time.
  3. Size. The sleeve's fixed dollar amount per stock, in whole shares, never more than 1% of the stock's average daily dollar volume (5% for two sleeves), and only if the sleeve's budget and the account's cash allow it.
  4. Capped limit order. One limit order, good for that day only, at the current price plus 1%, and never above a cap tied to the signal-day close. It never chases a stock that has run away.
  5. Fill. The broker fills the order (or it expires at the close). Lazarus matches every order and fill by its own order id, so a restart can never send a duplicate.
  6. Ledger. Every check, order, fill and exit is appended to a ledger in which each record carries a fingerprint of the one before it. Editing or deleting any past record breaks the chain, and the check on the Desk tab would show it.
  7. Broker check. After each check the desk compares its own book with each Alpaca account and raises an alert on any difference.

Selling. Each sleeve sells by its own research rules: a time exit after a set number of days, weeks or bars (sent at the check about 20 minutes before it is due), a stop loss for sleeves that have one, or Midna's own sell signal. Every sale is a day-only limit order at the latest price minus 1%. Depending on the sleeve, the holding time is counted from the fill or from the signal; each sleeve's own explanation says which.

Step 4

Two paper accounts

Main deskThe proven core: a handful of large sleeves (hourly swing, daily and weekly signals) on its own Alpaca paper account. It started with $100,000 on Oct 1, 2026.
Test deskThe lab: dozens of small sleeves testing new research ideas live, each with a small budget, on a second Alpaca paper account.

Both are paper accounts: the orders and fills are real broker simulations, the money is not.

Step 5

Staying up, backed up and published

WatchdogChecks every 60 seconds that the loop is running and has checked in within 45 minutes; if not, it restarts it. Only one copy can ever run.
Keep-aliveA routine that runs every 30 minutes on weekdays from 8:03 AM to 4:33 PM ET and starts the watchdog if the computer was restarted.
Backup and rebuildAt 4:55 PM and 8:45 PM ET, and right after any new order or fill, the desk's state and ledger are copied to a private GitHub repository. If the local state is ever lost, Lazarus restores it from there on startup, verifies the ledger chain, and re-checks both accounts at the broker.
Publishing12 times a day (9:40, 10:40, 11:40, 12:40, 1:40, 2:40, 3:40 … 5:00, 6:30, 8:30 ET) Lazarus rebuilds this page's data and the sleeve explanations from its live settings, checks them against a fixed data contract and scans them for secrets, then adds them to the site's GitHub repository. Nothing is ever deleted. The page reloads itself every 60 seconds.
